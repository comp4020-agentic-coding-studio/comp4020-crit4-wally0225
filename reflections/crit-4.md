# Crit 4: An instrument

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was defining an MVP before asking the agent to build
anything. My initial idea was bigger: all 26 letters, a different sound on
each, a more complex looper. Thinking about the brief's actual goal, I
realized I needed the smallest version that could still be expressive and
enjoyable — ten keys, ten unusual percussive sounds, a five-second recording
window, up to five overdub layers, and two controls: Record/Overdub and
Clear. With that scope fixed, I could give the agent specific instructions
instead of spending time on inessential features.

The other breakthrough was using my own experience of playing the instrument
as feedback. The agent could make the recording system technically work, but
it couldn't decide whether the interaction felt natural or the timing and
sounds were enjoyable. I had to play it myself, notice what felt confusing,
and turn that into new instructions — "your ear is the harness," made
practical.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This changed how I think about being a product manager. I used to think a
good product came from adding more features; this showed me that deciding
what not to build matters just as much. Thinking in MVP terms kept me focused
on the core experience — a stranger picking it up and playing it — rather
than technical impressiveness. I want to be someone who can identify that
essential experience, cut unnecessary scope, and work with an agent to turn
it into something usable, judged by how it feels to use rather than whether
it works. A strong MVP isn't a smaller version of a bigger product; it's a
focused version that tests whether the core idea is worth building at all.
